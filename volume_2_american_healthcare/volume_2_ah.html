Volume 2: The cult of American healthcare

an e-zine for heretics who want to laugh in these times of woe

You're American, regrettably, but it can't be helped. You are American but you don't live in the US. You are visiting for three weeks, so before even arriving in the country call your health insurance company to request a vacation override to stock up on your medication. You have made sure you have refills available, and ensure that your policy covers refills for up to 180 days of travel under the vacation override option. You check in on everything to make sure that your insurance will indeed approve the override.

 

After arriving to the US, you immediately call your insurance provider, whose CEO perished under extremely public circumstances, to request a vacation override and are approved. However, your doctor prescribed the wrong dosage of a medication, so you have to set up an appointment wherein you speak to a PA you've never heard of before so that the pharmacy sees the correct dosage.

 

You call the insurance provider back to set up the vacation override. They tell you they are not your primary insurance provider. You ask them to remove the other insurance on file and to mark themselves as the primary insurance, which they agree to do. They grant the vacation override. They also inform you that they are inputting another override to ensure that the insurer gives approval for your prescription while the change in primary insurance providers is processing, but say you will need another temporary override for primary insurance processing approval if you are unable to fill your medications by midnight.

 

But then, when you call the pharmacy, the pharmacy says you need to contact your doctor again because the quantity of refills is not correct. You have 1 refill of Sertraline 100 mg, 1 refill of prazosin, 1 refill of hydroxyzine, 1 refill of gabapentin. Your doctor needs to send 6 refills of each.

 

You call the doctor. The doctor's office says to message in whatever obscure healthcare messaging system they're using these days, so you do. The doctor texts you a list of the prescriptions they prescribed, just a regular SMS message. When you call about this, the doctor's office says there was no text and to message them in the portal. You message them in the portal. They say the doctor will text you soon. Two days later, you are still messaging them in the portal.

 

The doctor calls you for a telehealth appointment at dusk on the third day, or at least you think it's the doctor, but it turns out to be another PA you've never heard of before. He seems to have no idea that you've been trying to get help from their office for several days. The PA says the doctor does not usually do refills for longer than thirty days, and you explain that you will be on another continent and that the insurance has already approved this. The PA tries to bargain for 90 days, and you explain that you will be on another continent and that the insurance has already approved this. The PA continues trying to bargain.

 

Two hours later, your pharmacy informs you that you have 8 refills of Sertraline, 1 refill of prazosin, 3 refills of hydroxyzine, 9 refills of gabapentin. You ask them to please check that these are the correct numbers.

 

While you wait, you call the insurance provider back. Since it took several days to get things straight, you as the insurance provider to submit another temporary override for primary insurance processing approval. They confirm that they are your primary insurance and say there is no such thing as a temporary override for primary insurance processing approval, nor would one be needed if there were such a thing, because they are your primary insurer. You ask if there is anyone who might know what a temporary override for primary insurance processing approval is, and whether you can speak to them. You are transferred to a person who does not work for your insurance provider.

 

One hour later, your pharmacy informs you that you have 3 refills of Sertraline, 3 refills of prazosin, 3 refills of hydroxyzine, and 12 refills of gabapentin. You ask them to please check that these are the correct numbers, and they ask you to check that your primary insurance provider is really your primary insurance provider.

 

You confirm again with the primary insurance provider that they are your primary insurance provider.

 

Two hours later, your pharmacy informs you that you have  6 refills of Sertraline, 1 refills of prazosin, 8 refills of hydroxyzine, and 0 refills of gabapentin. You ask them to please check that these are the correct numbers.

 

Your pharmacy has closed.

 

You call your insurance provider, but the person on the other end of the line does not work for your insurance provider. She works for a shadowy prescription company that is a subsidiary of your insurance provider but cannot answer any questions about your insurance provider. She offers to give you a phone number for the insurance provider. You accept, but when you attempt to call your insurance provider, but the presence on the other end of the line does not work for your insurance provider.

 

The next morning, your pharmacy informs you that you have  18 refills of Sertraline, 2 refills of prazosin, 18 refills of hydroxyzine, and 24 refills of gabapentin. You ask them to please check ONLY the refills of the prazosin.
 

Two hours later, your pharmacy informs you that you have  2 refills of Sertraline, 2 refills of prazosin, 18 refills of hydroxyzine, and 18 refills of gabapentin. You ask them to please check that these are the right numbers

 

One hour later, your pharmacy informs you that you have entered the wrong insurance information and that the insurer with whom you approved the override was not your primary insurance provider, so you will have to start from scratch.

 

 

You begin to wonder what the point of this all is. If there are so many massive barriers to getting the medications that make you able to live a life without constant pain, why go on? Every call center is filled with people who are probably competent enough but whose competence is restrained by corporate compartmentalization. The more desperate you sound, the more they repeat company policy.

 

"Don't you understand that difficulties like this are why disabled people lose everything and become homeless simply trying to get medications their doctors have prescribed?" you ask.

 

"As I said," the voice on the phone says, then reiterates the arbitrary policy causing such confusion.

 

Your dear beloved spouse, concerned, calls your insurance provider. The insurance provider say that they are not your primary insurance provider. They say to call your primary insurance provider.

 

It turns out that unbeknownst to you, you have another insurance policy with another insurance company (one without a dead CEO). The insurance company with the dead CEO informs you that you will now need to process all of these prescriptions through this other insurance company, the one without a dead CEO, which has been your primary insurance the entire time. You try to call your primary insurance: they are closed.

 

When you attempt to call your primary insurance provider the next morning, they are unreachable until the afternoon because they're in another time zone. When you reach them, they begin the vacation override process. They ask where you're traveling as a prerequisite to approving the override, but have never heard of Chile. You repeat the name several times, even spelling it, but they cannot find any record of your destination. They ask where you're traveling. You spell Chile again. This time, it is found.

 

The person you've been speaking to is unable to approve the vacation override. They say you will need a preauthorization from your doctor. Your pharmacy texts to inform you that you have  6 refills of Sertraline, 6 refills of prazosin, 6 refills of hydroxyzine, and 6 refills of gabapentin. These are the correct numbers, but you cannot order them because your primary insurance (the one without the dead CEO) will not approve them. You ask why they are not your primary insurance, and ask them to please just end this insurance policy that you do not want and did not realize you had.

 

The insurance company without the dead CEO says that they will approve half of the vacation override. They tell you it will be three times more expensive than what the company with the dead CEO said they would charge you, and they will only give you half as much medication. They ask if this is acceptable. You ask them if you can cancel your policy so that the company with the dead CEO is your primary, because you can get the correct quantity for a price you can afford. The tell you that your policy ends in a week on Halloween. You ask them if you can cancel your insurance now, and they say no: you must keep this policy until Halloween, when the veil thins.

 

Your spouse again calls the pharmacy and asks them to delete any record of the insurance policy from the company without the dead CEO. They attempt to run the insurance request again. Your pharmacy informs you that you have  6 refills of Sertraline, 3 refills of prazosin, 6 refills of hydroxyzine, and 6 refills of gabapentin. You ask them to please check that these are the right numbers.

 

Your pharmacy informs you that you have  6 refills of Sertraline, 6 refills of prazosin, 6 refills of hydroxyzine, and 6 refills of gabapentin. They tell you that you cannot have this medication filled through the insurance company with the dead CEO because your primary insurance is through the company without the dead CEO.

 

 

The next morning, your spouse calls the company with the dead CEO and informs them that they are your primary insurance. They agree that this is true, and your spouse asks them to fill the prescription immediately, and they agree to do so.

 

That evening, the prescriptions appear in a bag on the outside of your bedroom door. It contains 6 refills of Sertraline, 6 refills of prazosin, 6 refills of hydroxyzine, and 6 refills of gabapentin. You pray to every power that exists to thank them for their divine intervention.
